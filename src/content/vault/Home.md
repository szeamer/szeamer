I'm Silvia Zeamer. I'm a software engineer who also holds in high regard good prose, fine arts and crafts, and nice lighting. I'm based in [Boston](https://www.reddit.com/r/boston/comments/1ljg3ay/why_is_boston_the_best_city_in_the_us/), MA, but sometimes in [Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_petroleum_industry_in_Norway), [Norway](https://www.visitnorway.com/places-to-go/fjord-norway/the-stavanger-region/). I believe deeply in the value of a vigorous liberal arts education, which I was lucky enough to receive at [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College). Having dedicated my career (or what I can presently foresee of my career) to building useful and interesting software, I distribute my free time between a perhaps inadvisably large array of contrasting activities:
- designing and making knitwear
- reading anything I encounter but especially literary fiction, essays, memoirs, and any large tome with 'magisterial' in the reviews on its jacket. Some of my all-time favourite books are:
	  - The Lord of the Rings by J.R.R. Tolkien
	  - A God in Ruins by Kate Atkinson
	  - Anya Csak Egy Van by Vámos Miklós
	  - The Year of Magical Thinking by Joan Didion
	  - A Suitable Boy by Vikram Seth
	  - Anna Karenina by Leo Tolstoy
- learning languages. I'm native in Hungarian and English, reasonably competent in French, rusty in Mandarin Chinese, and spent one very enjoyable semester taking Russian, which I hope to pick up again someday. Presently, I'm learning Norwegian and Bengali. I built an app to help me with that. If you're interested in language-learning methodology, please reach out. It's one of my great obsessions in life. Ha tudsz magyarul, nagyon boldogan ismerkednék. Nincsenek elegen magyarok az életemben sajnos.
- bookbinding
- birding & bird photography
- sitting in cafés with friends or anyone who seems nice
- spinning (yarn) (I have a spinning wheel and a drop spindle)
- DIY home improvement and building furniture. I'm very proud of my wallpaper application skills!

Professionally, I'm most energised by making sense of and building solutions to interesting problems, especially when I think those solutions will improve someone's life, and by collaborating with and mentoring thoughtful and compassionate people. I happily get to do lots of both those things at my job at [Tolemi](https://www.tolemi.com/)(I made our website! for fun). A disorganized list of technical interests:
- graphs and graph algorithms. i just think they're neat.
- large-scale data processing and data cleaning. combining disparate datasets and looking for what can be learned from them; scaling that up
- the Elixir programming language. it's both ergonomic and immensely capable, and the community around it is great. I had an amazing time [presenting at Elixir EU](https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=web&rct=j&opi=89978449&url=https://www.youtube.com/watch%3Fv%3DbVb1xmf3S34&ved=2ahUKEwjYscDekI6YAxU1MlkFHUjnELkQtwJ6BAgaEAI&usg=AOvVaw2i5BDCNmj06KZ0W6uN8q1K) this spring!
- Local LLMs and designing agents. I was and am suspicious of LLMs and the mania currently surrounding them, but I'm equally electrified by the opportunity they present to do fewer boring and tedious tasks in favour of more interesting tasks and more time spent talking to people. While I'm on this topic, I have to note my abhorrence of LLM-assisted communication. please, for the love of everything, just give me whatever typo-ridden bullet points you gave for a prompt rather than getting your model of choice to dress them up in three pages of dross. All text on this site was typed by me with my own fingers.

I grew up on a homestead on [the coast of Maine](https://www.surrymaine.gov/), which has made me excellent at electronics and survival skills, terrified of the global decline in insect populations, and possessed of a creeping conviction that air conditioning is an extraneous luxury. My advice to anyone with romantic thoughts of going off-grid in the northeast: don't. You can always go sit in a shelter in the woods. Electrifying your home which is located half a mile down a little-used logging road, which you will certainly find yourself wanting to do, will be more challenging. That's all about that.
